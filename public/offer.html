You enter one number. Everything else on this page is solved from the landed cost: the four prices, Shopify's cut at each of them, GST, the expected cost of returns, the ad budget each price can fund, and the most you can pay for a customer.
The tiers are the slice that survives. A rung is named for how much of each order is left before advertising: Excellent 65%, Good 55%, Average 45%, Poor 35% of revenue after GST. Those cut-offs come from the published benchmarks, where a kept margin of 20% plus ad spend of 25% of revenue is an average offer, and the top decile keeps 28%+.
Price is solved, not marked up. Shopify's fee, GST and returns all scale with the price, so a flat 3x on cost lands in the wrong place. The engine solves for the price where the maths actually closes.
Two things the margin alone will not tell you. A rung can look excellent and still be unaffordable, because the CPA you face is higher than the rung can fund. And a profitable day at $5,000 of spend can still sink you, because the stock behind it is paid for weeks before the money arrives. Shopify Payments Australia online card rates verified 4 Oct 2026 (Basic 1.75% + 30c, Grow 1.6% + 30c, Advanced 1.4% + 30c; Amex and international rates and the third-party gateway surcharge per the same published schedule). Tier cut-offs and the seeded CPA bands come from 2026 DTC and Meta-ads benchmark data: contribution-margin deciles (Level CFO, Commerce Catalyst), Meta purchase CPA by vertical (Triple Whale, AdLibrary), return rates and the cost of a return (ShipNetwork, 3PL Guys), and landed-cost composition (Syncost, Passport). CPA bands are US-sourced. The rate of CPA climb as spend rises has no published constant and is an operator dial, not a prediction.
